THE TOOLKIT

Thirty tools, one timeline

Each one does a job that used to need its own licence, its own machine and its own specialist.

GENERATIVE VIDEO

Up to eighteen seconds at 24fps from a prompt, an image, or a rough animatic you shot on a phone.

ROTOSCOPING

Track and isolate a subject across a shot in about nine seconds. It used to be an afternoon.

COLOUR

Match a grade across shots from a single reference frame, with the LUT exported for your finishing suite.

MOTION TRACKING

Planar and point tracking that survives motion blur, with data that exports to the usual compositors.

AUDIO CLEANUP

Separate dialogue from location noise, and remove a specific recurring sound without touching the rest.

COLLABORATION

Frame-accurate comments on a shared timeline, with versions kept rather than overwritten.

QUESTIONS

Who owns what I generate?

You do, on every paid plan, including commercial use. Free-plan output is watermarked and licensed for non-commercial use only.

How are credits consumed?

Only by generation, priced per second of output and per model. Rotoscoping, tracking, colour and audio tools do not consume credits on any plan.

What was the model trained on?

Licensed footage libraries and public data, with provenance stated per model in the research pages. Customer projects are never used for training.

Can I use it in a commercial production?

Yes, on Standard and above. Studio adds pooled credits, 4K exports and an indemnity clause that most productions' legal teams ask for.

Applying this system

  • Cinematic full-bleed photography and video are the primary UI elements. The interface is intentionally close to invisible.
  • One typeface does everything from 92px display to 11px labels. Hierarchy comes from weight and tracking, not from a second family.
  • Zero shadows and almost no borders — a single hairline (#27272a) is the only containment in the dark sections.
  • Display sits at line-height 1.0 with negative tracking; uppercase labels invert that with generous positive tracking at weight 450.
  • Dark cinematic bands alternate with paper-white reading sections. The white is cool-tinted (#e9ecf2), never pure.
