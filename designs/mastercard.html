WHAT WE DO

A network, not a bank

We do not issue the card or hold the account. We move the authorisation, the clearing and the settlement between the people who do.

Authorisation

A request routed from terminal to issuer and back in about four hundred milliseconds, at any hour, in any of two hundred and ten markets.

Fraud scoring

A risk decision computed on network-wide signal in the same round trip, so a legitimate customer is not stopped at the till.

Cross-border

Currency conversion at the network rate with the margin published, rather than folded invisibly into the exchange rate.

Tokenisation

The card number never reaches the merchant. A token scoped to that merchant does, and it is useless anywhere else.

Developer APIs

Sandbox credentials in about two minutes, and forty-one APIs documented with real request and response payloads.

Dispute resolution

A structured chargeback process with defined evidence requirements and a published clock at every stage.

Common questions

Do you issue cards?

No. Cards are issued by banks and licensed institutions. We operate the network that carries the authorisation, clearing and settlement between the issuer, the acquirer and the merchant.

How is interchange set?

By the network, published per region and per card category, and updated on a fixed annual schedule. It is paid by the acquirer to the issuer — we do not receive it.

What does tokenisation change?

The merchant stores a token scoped to them rather than the card number. If their systems are breached, the token cannot be used anywhere else, and the underlying card does not need reissuing.

How long do disputes take?

Each stage has a published clock — typically 30 days for the initial chargeback and 45 for representment. The tooling shows which stage a case is in and what evidence is still outstanding.

Applying this system

  • Extreme radius is the design language: 20px on body CTAs, 40px on cards, 99px and 1000px on pills and portraits. Anything square reads as a third-party embed.
  • The brand red and yellow belong to the logo mark only. Putting either on a button or a surface breaks the identity — the UI palette is cream, ink and one orange.
  • Signal orange is the single aggressive colour, reserved for consent actions and the eyebrow dot. It is a cue, never a fill.
  • Circular portraits sit on traced orbital arcs with small white satellite CTAs attached to their edge — the signature compositional move.
  • Ghost watermark headlines (cream on cream at heading scale) sit behind the circular portraits to add depth without adding contrast.
